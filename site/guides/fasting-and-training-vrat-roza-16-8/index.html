Fasting and training: vrat, roza and 16:8

By Dhruv Gupta, maker of Kynetix. 20 October 2026. 4 min read.

You can keep training while fasting if you move your session close to a meal, keep protein high in your eating window, and lighten the load on the hardest days. During vrat, dairy, paneer, peanuts and makhana carry the protein; during roza, split protein between sehri and iftar; on 16:8, fit two or three protein-rich meals into the window.

Navratri and Ekadashi vrat

A typical vrat skips grains and pulses, which removes dal, roti and rice. Protein then comes from dairy and nuts. Vrat-friendly foods in Kynetix's table:

Sabudana is mostly starch and oil, so it fills calories fast but adds little protein. Pair it with curd or paneer. Kynetix marks vrat-friendly foods and Kyno keeps its suggestions inside the rules of your fast.

Ramadan roza

  • Train shortly before iftar if you can, or an hour or two after it.
  • Split protein across sehri and iftar, with a protein snack later at night.
  • Hydrate through the evening; dehydration hurts strength more than hunger does.
  • Keep the weights, cut a set or two on the hardest days.

16:8 intermittent fasting

16:8 is simply a 16-hour gap and an 8-hour eating window. For lifting, put the window around your session, and fit two or three meals that each carry a solid protein source. Calories still decide fat loss; fasting is just a way to arrange them.

Safety first

If you have diabetes, take medication, are pregnant, or have a history of disordered eating, talk to a doctor before fasting and training together. Stop a session if you feel dizzy or faint.

Protein targets during a fast

Your protein target doesn't change because you're fasting; it just has to fit into fewer meals. On a vrat day without grains and pulses, that means leaning on dairy: a glass of milk in the morning, paneer at the main meal and curd with the evening snack can cover a large share. During Ramadan, aim for a solid protein source at both sehri and iftar.

What to eat around the session

  • Vrat: a light snack of fruit and curd or a glass of milk before training, and a paneer or curd based meal after.
  • Roza: if you train before iftar, keep it shorter; break the fast with water and dates, then a proper meal with protein.
  • 16:8: train late in your eating window or just before it opens, and eat a protein-rich meal soon after.

Adjusting the workout

Fasting days are not the time to chase personal records. Keep the main lifts at a weight you know, drop a set or two, and cut long finishers. Strength comes back quickly once you're eating normally again; staying consistent and injury-free matters more.

Hydration

Dehydration is the bigger risk, especially during roza in hot weather. Drink steadily through the non-fasting hours rather than all at once, and include some salt and potassium through food. Signs to stop a session: dizziness, headache, cramps or feeling faint.

Key takeaways

  • Train close to a meal and keep the session shorter on hard fasting days.
  • Keep your protein target; fit it into fewer, larger servings.
  • On vrat days, lean on paneer, curd, milk, peanuts and makhana.
  • Hydrate well in non-fasting hours, and stop if you feel faint.

Myths you can ignore

  • "You will lose all your muscle in a week of fasting." Short fasts with enough protein and some training barely touch it.
  • "16:8 burns more fat by itself." Fat loss still comes down to the calories in your eating window.

Questions

Can I go to the gym during Navratri fast?

Yes, for most healthy people. Train near a meal, keep protein from paneer, curd, milk and peanuts, and go lighter if you feel weak.

When should I work out during Ramadan?

Shortly before iftar, or an hour or two after it, so you can eat and drink soon after training.

Vrat mein protein kaise lein?

Paneer, dahi, doodh, moongphali aur makhana. 100 g paneer mein lagbhag 18 g protein.
