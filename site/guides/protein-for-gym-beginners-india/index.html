Protein for gym beginners in India

By Dhruv Gupta, maker of Kynetix. 20 October 2026. 4 min read.

As a gym beginner, aim for about 1.6 to 2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight a day: around 110 to 140 g if you weigh 70 kg. Eggs (13 g in two), chicken (46 g in 150 g grilled breast), paneer, soya and curd make it straightforward on Indian food.

How much you actually need

Protein needs follow your body weight and goal. Kynetix uses 1.8 g per kg on an average day for maintaining or gaining, a bit more on training days, and up to 2.2 g per kg while losing fat, on a weight capped at a BMI of 27 so extra body fat doesn't inflate the number. The protein calculator does it for you.

Two meals with chicken or fish and you are most of the way there.

If you eat eggs

  • 2 boiled eggs: 13 g protein, 155 kcal.
  • 4 egg whites: 14 g protein for only 68 kcal.
  • Egg bhurji (3 eggs): 19 g.

Whole eggs are fine; the yolk carries most of the vitamins. Use whites when you want more protein without the calories.

If you are vegetarian

Paneer (18 g per 100 g), soya chunks (26 g per 50 g dry), curd, milk and whey. The high-protein vegetarian guide has a full sample day.

Do beginners need whey?

No, but it is convenient. One scoop in water is about 24 g protein for 120 kcal. Think of it as food, not magic: if you already hit your number from meals, you don't need it.

Spread it over the day

Your body uses protein better when it arrives in a few decent servings rather than one huge meal. A practical target is three or four meals with a solid protein source each. For a 70 kg beginner, that might look like eggs or a cheela at breakfast, chicken or paneer at lunch, curd or whey in the evening and dal with a protein source at dinner.

A simple non-veg day

  • Breakfast: 2 boiled eggs (13 g) with toast and a glass of milk.
  • Lunch: 2 roti and a katori of chicken curry (19.5 g).
  • Evening: a scoop of whey (24 g) or a katori of curd.
  • Dinner: rice, dal and fish curry (18 g).

A simple egg-and-veg day

  • Breakfast: egg bhurji (19 g) with roti.
  • Lunch: rajma chawal and a katori of curd (5 g).
  • Evening: roasted chana (8 g).
  • Dinner: 2 roti with paneer bhurji (18.8 g).

Common beginner worries

Protein in normal food amounts is safe for healthy people; if you have kidney disease or another condition, ask your doctor first. More protein than you need doesn't build extra muscle, so there is no point going far above your target. And you don't need to eat within thirty minutes of training; the total for the day matters far more than the timing.

Myths you can ignore

  • "Protein powder is a steroid." It isn't. Whey is milk protein, dried. It is food in a tub.
  • "Vegetarians can't build muscle." Plenty do. It takes more planning, not more effort.
  • "You can only absorb 30 g at a time." Larger servings are still used; spreading protein out is simply more practical.
  • "More protein means more muscle." Only up to a point. Past your target, extra protein mostly becomes extra calories.

Key takeaways

  • Aim for about 1.6 to 2.0 g per kg of body weight a day, a little more while cutting.
  • Put a solid protein source in three or four meals.
  • Pick the sources you like and can afford; consistency matters most.
  • Track it for a couple of weeks to learn what your normal day actually gives you.

The habits that matter more

  • Protein at every meal, not only at dinner.
  • Consistency over perfection: close to your number most days beats perfect once a week.
  • Train with progressive overload; protein supports muscle you're giving a reason to grow.
  • Sleep. Recovery is when it happens.

Questions

How much protein should a gym beginner eat?

About 1.6 to 2.0 g per kg of body weight a day; for a 70 kg beginner that is roughly 110 to 140 g.

Is 2 eggs a day enough protein?

Two boiled eggs give about 13 g, a good start but well short of 100 g. Add other sources across the day.

Beginner ko kitna protein lena chahiye?

Lagbhag 1.6 se 2 g per kg body weight. 70 kg ho to roz 110 se 140 g.
